\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Visual and Point-Based Manipulation Policies}

Visual imitation learning provides a practical route from demonstrations to robot control.
ACT~\cite{zhao2023act} predicts action chunks for fine-grained manipulation, while Diffusion Policy~\cite{chi2023dp} models action sequences through conditional denoising.
At larger scale, $\pi_{0.5}$~\cite{physicalintelligence2025pi05} combines heterogeneous training data with vision-language-action modeling to support generalization to new environments.
These approaches establish strong image-conditioned policies, while 3D representations offer a complementary geometric interface.
DP3~\cite{ze2024dp3} conditions diffusion policies on compact features extracted from sparse scene point clouds.
Point Policy~\cite{haldar2025pp} represents observations and actions using semantic key points, enabling policy learning from human demonstration videos.
More recently, Point Bridge~\cite{haldar2026pb} uses unified point representations for synthetic-data training and sim-to-real transfer.
GraphPoint builds on this point-based perspective, organizing task-relevant observations by semantic entity roles and predicting actor point trajectories.
Our focus is how this organization supports instruction-dependent task composition.

\subsection{Object-Centric and Graph-Based Representations}

Object-centric representations expose individual entities as reusable components of a scene.
GROOT~\cite{zhu2023groot} combines segmented object point clouds with a transformer policy and segmentation correspondence to handle changes in backgrounds, viewpoints, and object instances.
Graph-based approaches further represent interactions between entities.
GraphMimic~\cite{chen2025graphmimic} abstracts videos into object and visual-action vertices, pretrains a model to generate future graphs, and conditions a control policy on these predictions.
Compose by Focus~\cite{qi2026compose} selects task-relevant scene subgraphs, encodes them with a graph attention network, and combines diffusion-based atomic skills with a vision-language task planner.
These works establish object structure and task-relevant relations as useful priors for control and skill composition.
GraphPoint studies a specific combination of actor/patient/target roles, explicit action-type and modifier conditioning, and point-trajectory generation.
Its distinction lies in this representation and policy interface; object-centric inputs and graph-based skill composition are established ingredients.

\subsection{Compositional Generalization in Manipulation}

Compositional generalization concerns new combinations of familiar task factors as well as new sequences of learned behaviors.
CLIPort~\cite{shridhar2022cliport} couples pretrained semantic features with spatially precise action prediction for language-conditioned manipulation.
VIMA~\cite{jiang2023vima} introduces multimodal task prompts and a benchmark with 4 generalization levels, while CompoSuite~\cite{mendez2022composuite} explicitly combines robots, objects, obstacles, and objectives to evaluate unseen task combinations.
LIBERO~\cite{liu2023libero} studies declarative and procedural knowledge transfer across manipulation tasks, and CALVIN~\cite{mees2022calvin} evaluates language-conditioned continuous control over multi-step task sequences.
These benchmarks address complementary forms of transfer whose difficulty depends on the factors held out during training.
CoMani provides a focused LIBERO-based evaluation of modifier combinations at a fixed action type, action-type combinations at a fixed modifier, and unseen sequences of atomic tasks.
Its Seq-ID and Seq-OOD sets distinguish composition of familiar atomic tasks from sequences containing a held-out atomic combination, while matched initial scenes reduce opportunities to infer the task from visual context alone.
