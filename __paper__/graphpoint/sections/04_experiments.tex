\section{Experiments}

We evaluate GraphPoint on CoMani-Mod, CoMani-Act, and CoMani-Seq using the task splits and protocols in Sec.~\ref{sec:benchmark}.
We report success rates separately for ID and OOD tasks over 30 trials per atomic task and 10 trials per sequence.
For the reported atomic-task experiments, GraphPoint is trained with 10 demonstrations per ID task and is evaluated at 30,000 training steps.
Tables report mean task success rates; blank entries indicate evaluations that are not yet complete.
Our experiments address 4 questions:
\par\noindent\textbf{Q1:} Can GraphPoint generalize across modifiers?
\par\noindent\textbf{Q2:} Can GraphPoint generalize across actions?
\par\noindent\textbf{Q3:} Can GraphPoint compose atomic skills?
\par\noindent\textbf{Q4:} Which components matter?
\par
We answer the first 3 questions through comparisons on the corresponding suites and the fourth through ablations on CoMani-Mod.

\subsection{Can GraphPoint Generalize Across Modifiers?}
We compare GraphPoint with ACT~\cite{zhao2023act}, Diffusion Policy (DP)~\cite{chi2023dp}, DP3~\cite{ze2024dp3}, PointPolicy~\cite{haldar2025pp}, $\pi_{0.5}$~\cite{physicalintelligence2025pi05}, and PointBridge~\cite{haldar2026pb}; the ID evaluation of PointBridge remains in progress.
As shown in Table~\ref{tab:modifier_comparison}, GraphPoint achieves 96.39\% ID and 75.56\% OOD success, exceeding the highest baseline ID success ($\pi_{0.5}$, 91.11\%) by 5.3 percentage points and the highest baseline OOD success (DP3, 57.22\%) by 18.3 points. $\pi_{0.5}$ obtains 48.33\% OOD success.
ACT, DP, and PointPolicy also have low ID success in this setting, so their OOD results reflect limitations in both task acquisition and generalization.
The comparisons with DP3 and $\pi_{0.5}$ provide references with stronger ID performance for assessing held-out modifier combinations.

GraphPoint achieves at least 63.33\% success on every OOD task, whereas DP3 ranges from 16.67\% to 93.33\%.
Its gains are particularly large for placing the wine bottle on the cabinet (73.33\% versus 16.67\%) and to the right of the plate (66.67\% versus 23.33\%).
However, DP3 performs better on placing cream cheese to the right of the cabinet (93.33\% versus 76.67\%).
These results support broader coverage of held-out relations by GraphPoint, while the remaining ID--OOD gap shows that modifier composition is still challenging.

\subsection{Can GraphPoint Generalize Across Actions?}
Table~\ref{tab:action_comparison} compares GraphPoint with DP3 on CoMani-Act, with $\pi_{0.5}$ left pending.
GraphPoint reaches 90.48\% ID and 64.44\% OOD success, compared with 83.33\% and 60.00\% for DP3.
The modest average OOD advantage masks substantial differences across held-out tasks.
GraphPoint transfers rotation from the stove knob to cream cheese with 76.67\% success, while DP3 obtains 0\%.
Conversely, GraphPoint trails DP3 on putting cream cheese to the right of the stove (26.67\% versus 80.00\%) and sweeping the bowl to the right of the plate (90.00\% versus 100.00\%).
Thus, these results support cross-entity rotation transfer but do not establish a uniform advantage across action combinations.
For put/sweep, the reported metric verifies the final spatial goal and does not independently verify the interaction mode.

\subsection{Can GraphPoint Compose Atomic Skills?}
We compare GraphPoint with DP3 and $\pi_{0.5}$ on CoMani-Seq to assess execution of 3-step instructions using policies trained on atomic-task demonstrations, without sequence-level demonstrations.
Table~\ref{tab:sequence_comparison} reports success rate (SR) and progress rate (PR) over 10 trials per sequence, together with their means across all 6 sequences.
Tasks 0--1 test composition of 3 ID skills, tasks 2--3 introduce 1 OOD atomic task, and tasks 4--5 contain 1 and 2 OOD atomic tasks, respectively.
The final pair tests the effect of replacing an ID atomic task with its OOD modifier variant while keeping the remaining subtasks and their order fixed.
We compare environment-based switching (ES) with self-switching (SS), where GraphPoint triggers subtask transitions using its own predicted progress.
The standard progress head uses patient and target summaries (P+T).
GraphPoint achieves 51.67\% SR and 79.44\% PR with environment-based switching, and 38.33\% SR and 78.33\% PR with self-switching.
Thus, self-switching reduces SR by 13.33 percentage points while PR decreases by 1.11 points, indicating that similar partial progress does not guarantee completion of the full sequence.
DP3 obtains 0\% SR and 11.11\% PR even with environment-based switching.
% Including actor summaries (A+P+T) yields 41.67\% SR and 74.44\% PR under self-switching, compared with 38.33\% and 78.33\% for P+T. The mixed changes do not establish a consistent advantage for either progress-head input on these 60 trials.
The $\pi_{0.5}$ evaluation remains pending.

\textbf{Geometry and graph structure.}
Removing RPC reduces OOD success by 31.1 percentage points, compared with a 5.8-point ID decrease, consistent with its intended role in reducing dependence on detailed training geometry.
Full graph connectivity retains 95.28\% ID success but lowers OOD success to 63.89\%, supporting patient-mediated interaction as a useful inductive bias.
Merging all entity points yields 27.78\% OOD success despite retaining 12 summary tokens in total.
This variant jointly removes role embeddings, separate local entity groups, and chain connectivity, and reads shared summaries for progress; it therefore tests the structured encoder as a whole rather than any single component.

\textbf{Coordinate and output representations.}
Removing current-TCP centering lowers OOD success to 25.00\% while retaining 92.50\% ID success, indicating that relative geometry is particularly useful for held-out combinations.
Replacing point trajectories with Cartesian delta actions gives 95.00\% ID and 73.33\% OOD success, close to the full model.
The observed gains are therefore more pronounced for relative coordinates, geometric augmentation, and role structure than for point output alone.
These comparisons describe the reported checkpoint evaluations; repeated-training estimates are needed to assess the reliability of small differences.
